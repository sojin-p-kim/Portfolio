About

I'm a prospective mechanical engineering PhD applicant working at the intersection of robotics, biomechanics, and wearable sensing. My goal is to develop quantitative, clinically deployable methods for measuring human and robotic joint motion — particularly sensor-based, non-invasive approaches that move joint kinematics measurement outside the traditional motion-capture lab.

Currently I'm an exoskeleton intern at the Institute for Human and Machine Cognition (IHMC), where I've built a wireless IMU motion-capture system and developed a real-time state estimator for a bipedal humanoid robot. Previously I completed research on gait and bimanual coordination at the UW–Madison Motor Behavior Lab, and earned my MS in Mechanical Engineering from UW–Madison.

Projects

IHMC · 2026

Wireless IMU Motion Capture System

A four-sensor wireless IMU system that tracks lower-limb joint angles in real time. Each node pairs an ESP32C3 microcontroller with an MPU9250 9-axis IMU, streaming orientation data at 330 Hz over WiFi/UDP to a live MuJoCo skeletal model that computes hip, knee, and ankle angles with no wired connection to the body.

ESP32 MPU9250 Madgwick fusion PCA calibration MuJoCo Python UDP
  • Designed and fabricated compact enclosures integrating IMU, microcontroller, antenna, and power
  • Implemented static, functional (PCA-based axis finding), and simultaneous zero calibration pipeline
  • Validated knee flexion angle against OpenSim reference, showing repeatable gait-like tracking
IHMC · 2026

Right-Invariant EKF for Humanoid Robot State Estimation

A real-time SE₂(3)-based Right-Invariant Extended Kalman Filter for bipedal robot locomotion, fusing IMU, joint encoders, and contact constraints to estimate the full body state of the ALEX V2 humanoid during walking.

InEKF SE₂(3) Lie groups Python MuJoCo Signal processing
  • Built a Python replay and validation pipeline for 1 kHz walking datasets
  • Automated sensor verification, filter consistency testing, and estimator benchmarking
  • Developed a MuJoCo ground-truth simulator to validate filter performance against known kinematics

Automated identification of anatomical landmarks on the calcaneus from 3D CT-derived surface models, supporting research into subtalar joint mechanics following ankle arthrodesis in osteoarthritis patients.

Surface analysis CT point clouds MATLAB 3D visualization

Speed Optimization of Robot Motion via Data Point Reduction

MATLAB Motion capture Numerical methods
