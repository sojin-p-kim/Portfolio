VR Bimanual Coordination & Gait Study

Motor Behavior Lab · UW–Madison · 2025–2026 · PI: Dr. Andrea Mason
UnityC#HTC Vive ProLeap MotionMotion CaptureForce Plates

Overview

This project investigated how visual feedback of each hand affects bimanual grasp coordination, and whether walking performance differs between VR and real environments. It was conducted in the Motor Behavior Lab at UW–Madison, which studies how people coordinate body movements and use visual cues to grasp objects and navigate.

VR environment

  • Built a Unity VR environment using an HTC Vive Pro headset
  • Integrated Leap Motion hand tracking so participants could see and use their own hands in VR
  • Designed experimental conditions that selectively showed or hid visual feedback of each hand during bimanual grasping tasks

Research context

Understanding how VR alters natural movement is important for rehabilitation applications — if VR changes how people walk or reach, those differences need to be accounted for when designing VR-based therapy. This work contributes to the lab's broader goal of understanding visuomotor coordination during everyday tasks like grasping and walking.
